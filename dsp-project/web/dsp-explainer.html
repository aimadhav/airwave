A VISUAL INTRODUCTION · NO DSP BACKGROUND REQUIRED

How does a recording become
something an AI can trust?

The DSP layer is a sequence of careful translations. It takes a microphone signal, makes it comparable, finds the parts that contain speech, attaches time and speaker information, and hands the result to the AI layer.

DSP, in one sentenceDigital Signal Processing is the use of measurements and algorithms to turn a physical signal into useful digital information.

THE WHOLE JOURNEY

A recording travels through seven questions.

Read it as a chain of evidence: every later answer should be traceable back to a time range in the original recording.

THE TECHNIQUES, SEPARATED

Five tools answer five different questions.

These words are often mixed together. They are not the same algorithm. Think of them as different workers passing the same recording down a line.

VAD · USED NOW

Voice Activity Detection

“Is there likely speech in this moment?”

Our energy VAD measures RMS in 30 ms frames, converts it to decibels, compares it with an adaptive noise threshold, then joins nearby active frames.

Speaker diarization

“Which speech regions belong to the same voice?”

Real multi-speaker mode uses a learned segmentation model to find speech, TitaNet to turn each voice region into a numerical fingerprint, and clustering to group similar fingerprints. The one-speaker fallback and mock fixture remain separate options.

Speaker identification

“Can this voice be linked to a known person?”

This is different from diarization. A voice group is not automatically Alice or Bob. Names need supplied evidence, such as native Meet metadata or registered profiles. Unregistered acoustic labels remain anonymous, even if transcription mentions a name. Acoustic voice-profile matching is a future feature, not an implemented part of this MVP.

01

REPRESENTATION

First, sound becomes numbers.

A computer cannot store “Alice said hello.” It first receives measurements of air pressure.

A microphone measures air movement
The vertical value is signal amplitude; the horizontal value is time. Dots show individual measurements. This sketch displays only a few dots for clarity; 16 kHz audio has 16,000 every second.
Analogy: a flipbook. Each sample is one tiny picture of the sound. Many pictures played in order create the motion we hear.

Two numbers to know

Sample rate
How many measurements we take per second. 16 kHz = 16,000 samples/second.
Amplitude
How far the signal is from its middle line. Larger values usually mean more energy.
WHY THIS STEP EXISTS

Once sound is represented as numbers, the same operations can be measured, tested and repeated.

03

SHORT-TIME ANALYSIS

We do not judge four minutes at once.

The signal is inspected in small overlapping windows because speech changes quickly.

0 ms1020304050607080
30 ms framethe small piece we inspect
10 ms hophow far we move before looking again

Frames overlap so the energy estimate is updated every 10 ms. Clip boundaries are chosen later from active frames; framing alone does not prevent a word from being cut.

RMS = √(x₁² + x₂² + … + xₙ²) / √nPlain English: measure typical amplitude in a window. Mean square is the average squared sample value; RMS is its square root.
Why not inspect one sample?One sample is too tiny to say anything useful about speech. A short window gives us enough context to estimate energy while still following changes in the voice.

VOICE ACTIVITY DETECTION · VAD

Now we ask: “voice or not voice?”

VAD finds candidate speech regions. It does not transcribe words and it does not identify a human being.

OUR IMPROVEMENT

We compare frame level with max(−35 dBFS, noise floor + 3 dB). The floor is the 10th percentile of frame levels. Gaps up to 250 ms are joined; speech shorter than 200 ms is discarded. Noise and music can still trigger this energy detector.

IMPLEMENTED · REAL MULTI-SPEAKER MODE

Different sentences can still belong to the same voice.

VAD finds speech. Voice embeddings and clustering decide which regions sound like the same speaker. These are separate jobs.

i

This drawing is intuition, not a model plot. Real fingerprints are high-dimensional vectors. Our runtime normalizes them, compares cosine distance, and groups them with complete-linkage clustering. A threshold that is too strict creates extra speakers; too loose can merge different people.

How do we know it works?Count is not enough

On a separate 30-second recording with annotated overlaps, speaker-time error was 5.99%, compared with 49.57% for the one-speaker fallback. That uses no boundary tolerance. It is a small development test, not a universal accuracy score. Noise raised the error to 10.14%.

∞

HONEST LIMITS

What this pipeline does—and does not—claim.

It does
  • standardize audio and detect likely speech;
  • preserve timestamps, overlaps and provenance;
  • keep unknown speakers anonymous;
  • produce repeatable AI-ready clips.
It does not yet
  • prove a real name from voice alone;
  • separate overlapping speakers with source separation;
  • download Meet artifacts through OAuth;
  • guarantee perfect labels, words, or speaker counts on every recording.

THE SHORT VERSION

Measure first.
Label honestly.
Hand over evidence.

That is the DSP section: not a black box, but a visible chain of transformations that makes the AI layer safer and easier to test.
